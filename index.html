This page sits between partners who work in the same place or on the same natural asset, and between them and people who can help (funders, researchers, practitioners, job seekers), so that they can find each other without waiting for an introduction. It is a diagnostic, and it is meant to be temporary.

That sentence follows the Foundation's own test for a shared tool, set out in its practice notes: name the sides, say what crosses, and say how long it lives. The same notes ask to "measure the gaps actually crossed, not the count of partners", which is why the headline numbers here are about exchanges and replies.

How partners are grouped

  • Focus area: the four the Foundation names (Urban, Rural, Conservation and Restoration, Messaging), plus one cross-cutting bucket for policy, funding and data organisations.
  • Natural assets: the six the Foundation names (air, water, soil, biodiversity, energy, materials). A partner carries an asset only when the Foundation's description of it points to one; partners carry none.
  • Role (eight kinds of organisation), rolled up into four contributions that loosely follow the Foundation's three kinds of capital: place-based, narrative (social), connective (network) and financial.
  • All three are an editorial reading of the Foundation's description of each partner, kept in one open file, data/registry.json. They are a way to find people, not an official taxonomy.

Where a partner is placed

  • Stated (filled pin): the partner's name or the Foundation's description names the state or a place inside it. The pin sits on the named place, or in the middle of the state when only the state is named.
  • Stated on the partner's own website (filled pin): one line per state, with the first place the site names and a short quote, kept with its source page in data/workplaces.json. Story datelines, events and research about a state are left out. This is a one-time read, not refreshed daily.
  • Office (hollow pin): the main office given on the partner's own website, looked up only for partners with no stated place, with the source page linked on each partner's card. Offices are not counted as places of work anywhere on this page.
  • Declared: the partner has corrected its own entry, with a public link.
  • Indicative (✳, off by default): states named at least times in the partner's own Grove posts. This is where a partner writes about, which is not always where it works.
  • Partners described as national, regional or with no place stated are never spread across states.
  • The base map is OpenStreetMap data, served without a key by OpenFreeMap and limited to India. Place names are located with OpenStreetMap's Nominatim. Pins that share a town are fanned out in a ring so each can be clicked.

How the numbers are made

  • Update threads in The Grove's Partner Updates category are matched to partners by the account that opened the thread, or by the organisation's name in the title. An account whose matched threads all belong to one partner is treated as that partner's.
  • What is happening: partners' own posts on The Grove since 2024 were read for the work they describe: what is done, the numbers, who it is done with (collaborators, government bodies, funders) and what is asked for. Each item links to its post and carries a verbatim quote. Posts were read up to their first 4,000 characters. Replies and reply counts are not used anywhere.
  • Readings and collaborations are written from those items and are interpretation. A collaboration is a proposal, not something the partners have agreed.
  • Calls, jobs and events: topics started in the last days in the Funding, Collaboration, Small grants, Green Jobs and Events categories, from accounts past the forum's new-user level. Dates of the events themselves are not read.

The state of the place

    These are read next to partner presence, never combined into a score. A next action is suggested only from a rule that is written down: a shared asset with no exchange, an update with no reply, a stressed asset with no partner, or a partner based in a state with no stated place of work.
